title: "Evaluate, Detect, Adapt: Trustworthy Deployment of Large Behavior Models"
abstract: "Large-scale robot learning, such as using large behavior models (LBMs), has increasingly become the norm in robot learning literature. Nevertheless, many questions remain about how to deploy these embodied learning-based systems in the real world. First, we need a rigorous and efficient means to evaluate candidate robot learning models with statistical guarantees. Second, to deploy these models in human environments, they should be equipped with reliable failure detection systems despite the challenge posed by countless and often unforeseeable failure types and conditions during deployment. Lastly, these models should have the capacity to explore and adapt to new environments, preferences, and tasks. In this talk, I will overview our work along these research directions as part of the Trustworthy Learning under Uncertainty (TLU) team at TRI, focusing on rigorous and fast sequential evaluation, detecting failures on the fly, and adapting large behavior models to new tasks during deployment. These research lines aim to spur algorithmic development for robust and reliable deployment of embodied foundation models in human environments. To learn more about our work, visit: https://www.tri.global/trustworthy-learning-under-uncertainty-tlu."
